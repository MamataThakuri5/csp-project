Types of chemicals

Farmers use these to protect crops and raise yield. Each group behaves differently on and around produce.

Pests

Insecticides

Target insects. Toxicity and how long they last vary widely.

Weeds

Herbicides

Control weeds. Some leave residues on surfaces or in soil.

Molds

Fungicides

Protect against molds and blights. Can be applied close to harvest.

Nutrients

Fertilizers

Not pesticides, but they influence uptake and residue behaviour.

Regulators set Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs), the highest residue allowed after a risk assessment.

Health effects

Effects depend on the chemical, the dose, how it enters the body, and how long exposure lasts.

Short term

Acute

Headache, nausea, skin irritation, or worse after high-dose incidents. Mostly affects people who apply chemicals, or accidental exposure.

Long term

Chronic

Low-level exposure over time is the main consumer concern. Where studied, links include hormone disruption, effects on child development, and higher cancer risk for certain compounds.

Children and pregnant people are more sensitive, so reducing exposure matters most for them.

Identify and avoid

You cannot judge residues by looking at produce. These steps help more.

Detection is not danger

A swab test may show a residue is present. Only the amount compared with the legal limit (MRL) tells you if it is a problem.

Read labels

Look for certified organic marks or post-harvest treatment labels.

Ask vendors

Ask about the waiting period between the last spray and harvest, and how produce was handled.

Buy in season

Local, seasonal produce often needs fewer treatments to store and travel.

Safe washing

Pick your produce. Wash and scrub came out best in our workshop.

Our findings: wash and scrub reduced visible residue most on smooth-skinned produce. Peeling worked but removes nutrients. Vinegar soaking gave only a modest extra benefit. This is a practical activity, not a laboratory analysis.

Organic vs conventional

Organic is not residue-free, and conventional is not automatically unsafe. Wash both.

OrganicConventional
Chemicals allowedMostly natural or approved low-impact productsWider range of synthetic products within legal limits
Typical residuesUsually lower, not zeroPresent at or below MRLs when rules are followed
Cost and accessOften higher priced, harder to findAffordable and widely available
What to doWash and scrub either type before eating.
